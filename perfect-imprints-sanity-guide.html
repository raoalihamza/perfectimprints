1. Sanity basics (start here)

Your website has two halves. The public website is what your customers see. Sanity Studio is the private dashboard where you change the words, images, products, links, and pages. You do not touch any code. You fill in fields and click Publish, and the website updates by itself.

Opening the Studio and logging in

The Studio lives at a private web address (your developer will give you the exact link, it ends in /admin3773752). Open it in your browser and log in. Once you are in, you will see a list on the left called Content. That list is your table of contents for the whole website.

The Content list (the left menu)

Everything you can edit is one of the items in that left list: Home Page, Global Settings, Mega Menu, Custom Category, Category Override, Product Placement, Custom Product, Blog Post, Blog Category, Author, FAQ, Video, Brand, Lead Submission, Page (and one item, Curated Category, that you should ignore). The rest of this guide walks through each one.

Two kinds of items: singletons and collections

  • Singletons are one-of-a-kind pages. There is exactly one Home Page, one Global Settings, one Mega Menu. You click it and edit the one document.
  • Collections are lists you add to. Blog Post, Custom Category, Brand, FAQ, and so on each hold many documents. You click the menu item, then either open an existing document or click the + (or "Create new") to add another.

The single most important idea: Draft vs Published

Read this twice

When you edit something, your changes are saved as a Draft automatically, but the public website does not change yet. The website only updates when you press the blue Publish button (bottom-right of the document).

So the rule is simple: edit, then Publish. If you ever change something and "it didn't update on the site", 99% of the time it is because you forgot to Publish.

A document with unpublished changes usually shows a small "Draft" or orange marker. After you Publish, that marker clears. You can keep a draft as long as you like and Publish later, which is handy for preparing something before it goes live.

How a change reaches the live site

After you Publish, the website rebuilds the affected page automatically, usually within a minute. You do not need to call your developer, and nothing needs to be "deployed". Publish is all it takes.

Editing fields

Inside a document you will see labelled boxes (fields). Some are simple text, some are rich text (where you can make words bold or add a link), some are lists you can drag to reorder, and some are images. A few useful habits:

  • Rich text links: select the words you want to turn into a link, then click the link button in the little toolbar and type the address (for example /rush-products for a page on your own site, or a full https://... address for an outside site). Do not type the link as raw code like <a href...>; use the link button instead.
  • Images: most image fields let you either upload a file or, in some places, paste an image URL. Uploading is preferred. To swap an image, open the field and choose a new file.
  • Lists: drag the handle on the left of a row to reorder. Use the + or "Add item" to add. Click the three dots or the X to remove.
  • Hide instead of delete: in several places (the page builder especially) there is a "Hidden" toggle. Turning it on removes a section from the live page without deleting your work, so you can bring it back later.

The three tabs at the very top

  • Structure is the normal editing view (the Content list). You will be here almost all the time.
  • Vision is a technical query tool. Ignore it. You will never need it.
  • Push to Sanity is a special tool for taking over one of your existing category pages so you can edit it. It has its own section below (jump to it).

2. Home Page

This singleton controls your homepage, top to bottom. Open Content › Home Page. The fields, in roughly the order they appear on the page:

Hero (the big text at the top)

Three editable pieces: a small eyebrow line ("BULK PROMOTIONAL EXPERTS SINCE 1999"), the big headline ("Custom Promotional Products That People Actually Use"), and the sub-paragraph below it. Change the words here and Publish. (There is also an older image-banner field kept as a fallback; you do not need it.)

Banner Row heading + the three banners

Just under the hero is a row of up to three image banners (Sunglasses, Event Tents, Hats, by default). You can edit:

  • The heading above the row ("Featured Product Categories") and its sub-line.
  • Each banner: its image, the link it goes to (for example /cat/sunglasses), and its alt text. Swap any image, change any link, or remove a banner. An empty row simply shows nothing.

Value Pillars (the three feature boxes)

The boxes like "Bulk Pricing on 22,000+ Products" and "Rush Production Available". Each has a heading and a body. The body is rich text, so you can add links inside it (select the words, click the link button). If you ever add a fourth or more pillar, the row automatically turns into a rotating carousel showing three at a time.

Testimonials + heading

The customer quotes section. You can edit the heading ("What Our Customers are Saying") and the list of testimonials (each has the quote, the person's name, and their title/company). The section shows three at a time and scrolls/rotates automatically when there are more than three.

Section headings for the product rows

There are editable headings for the "New and Trending Promotional Products" row and the "Rush Production Promotional Products" row. The products in those rows are pulled in automatically; you control the headings here.

Bottom SEO text

The long paragraph block near the footer ("Perfect Imprints has been helping businesses..."). It is rich text and spans the full width; links inside it show in red.

Homepage meta title and description

The title and description Google shows for your homepage in search results. Pre-set to your approved copy; edit if you wish, keeping the title short (around 60 characters) and the description around 155 characters.

3. Global Settings

This singleton holds the things that appear across the whole site (header, footer, contact details). Change something here and it updates everywhere at once. Open Content › Global Settings.

Contact details + hours

Your phone number, email, mailing address, and hours of operation ("M-F 9:00 AM-5:00 PM EST"). These feed the footer, the contact areas, and the business information Google reads about you. Update them here in one place.

Social Links

Your social media profiles (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook, X, YouTube, Pinterest). For each one you set the platform, paste the profile URL, and toggle it on. The matching icon appears automatically. Turn one off and it disappears from the footer; there are no dead placeholder links.

Footer Columns (the three link columns)

The footer's three link columns (About Us, Popular Links, Customer Service) are fully editable here. Change a label, change where a link points, add or remove links, or reorder them. The Contact column and the social icons are handled separately (above), not in this list.

For an internal link (a page on your own site) you can type the address with or without the leading slash — both /llm-info-perfect-imprints and llm-info-perfect-imprints work; the slash is added for you. An external link (an outside site) must include the full https://... address.

Copyright text

The line at the very bottom. Use (year) as a placeholder and it is replaced with the current year automatically.

CTA Banner

Default wording for the call-to-action banner used in places around the site (heading, body, button label and link).

Deals / New Products / Rush Products page controls

Each of these three aggregator pages has a small set of controls here:

  • Heading, intro paragraph, meta title and description for the page.
  • Hide these SKUs: paste a product's SKU to remove it from that page's grid.
  • Pin these SKUs: paste a SKU to force a product onto that page even if the automatic feed missed it.

The products on these three pages are refreshed automatically from Geiger every week. These controls let you nudge what shows without anyone touching the feed.

4. Mega Menu (the top navigation)

This singleton is your main website menu, the bar across the top (Shop by, All Categories, New Products, Rush Products, Deals, Brands, Services, Videos, Blog). Open Content › Mega Menu. The Items list is the menu, left to right; drag to reorder, and the live header follows.

Each item has a Type:

  • Plain link: a single clickable word with one address (New Products, Deals, Brands, Videos, Blog).
  • Dropdown (flat list): a simple drop-down of links (Services: Kitting, Company Stores, and so on).
  • Mega panel (columns): the big multi-column panels (Shop by, All Categories). These have columns, each with a header and a list of links.

You can rename items, change where they link, hide them, add new ones, or reorder them. For most day-to-day needs you will only ever tweak a label or a link here.

As with the footer, an internal link can be typed with or without the leading slash (/blog or blog both work); an external link must include the full https://... address.

Note

The two big panels (Shop by, All Categories) already contain hundreds of category links generated from your full catalog. You can edit them, but you usually will not need to. Be careful not to delete a whole panel by accident.

5. Your category pages, and the three tools that control them

This is the most important section to understand, because it is where most of your day-to-day product work happens, and there are three related tools that can confuse newcomers. Read this overview first, then the three tools that follow.

How category pages work by default

You have about 22,180 category pages (like /cat/water-bottles or /cat/backpacks). They were generated automatically, with their heading, intro text, buying guide, FAQs, and product grid already built in. You do not need to touch most of them. They run themselves and pull live products from Geiger.

You only step in when you want to change one: edit its words, add or remove specific products, take it over completely, or build a brand-new category page that does not exist yet. That is what the three tools are for.

The three tools at a glance

Tool Think of it as Use it when you want to…
Custom Category Taking over (or creating) a whole page Edit the words, heading, intro, buying guide, FAQs, and product list of a page, or build a brand-new category page from scratch.
Category Override A light touch-up on one existing page Just add or remove a few products on a page, or force a page to show the contact form instead of products, without rewriting the whole page.
Product Placement Moving one product around Put one specific product into several categories at once (or remove it from several), working from the product's side instead of the page's side.
Simple rule of thumb

Editing the page's words/content? Use Custom Category (usually via Push to Sanity).
Just adding or removing a few products on one page? Use Category Override.
One product needs to appear in many pages? Use Product Placement.

6. Custom Category (take over or create a page)

A Custom Category document is a full category page that you control. When you publish one, it takes over its web address (for example, a Custom Category with slug backpacks takes over /cat/backpacks) and the page is now driven by what you typed, not the automatic version. Delete or unpublish it, and the page falls back to the automatic version. Nothing is lost.

Two ways to make one:

  • To take over a page that already exists (like /cat/backpacks): use the Push to Sanity tool. It creates the Custom Category for you already filled in with the current heading, text, FAQs, and products, so you start from what is live and just edit. This is the recommended path.
  • To build a brand-new page that does not exist yet: open Content › Custom Category, click Create new, and fill it in from scratch.

The fields you will see

  • Title / heading (H1): the big page title.
  • Slug: the web address segment after /cat/. Pick carefully; this is the page's address.
  • SEO (meta title and description): what Google shows in search results.
  • Intro: the lead-in paragraph under the heading (rich text).
  • Body / buying guide: the longer content lower on the page (rich text, with headings).
  • FAQs: question-and-answer pairs that appear on the page (and help your SEO).
  • Products: the product list for the page. You add products with the searchable picker (type a product name, SKU, or brand and click to add). You can also add Custom Products here.
  • External link (optional): if set, the page's buttons point there; if left blank, buttons point to your contact form.

Editing, then publishing

  1. Open the Custom Category (or create it via Push to Sanity).
  2. Edit any fields you want: heading, intro, buying guide, FAQs, products.
  3. Optionally use the Generate with AI button to draft the text for you.
  4. Review everything, especially the product list.
  5. Click Publish. The page is now live with your version.
Important: the "full-capped-60" categories

A small number of categories (around 65, such as belt-buckles) do not have a clean set of Geiger products, so the automatic system quietly hid an off-topic fallback set of about 60 products behind a "show the contact form instead" rule. That is fine while the page runs automatically.

But the moment you take such a page over with Push to Sanity, those ~60 off-topic products get copied into your product list and the "hide" rule no longer applies, so they become visible. Before you Publish a page like this, look through the Products list and remove anything off-topic (or turn on Force CTA / leave the external link blank so it shows the contact form instead). Always review the product list before publishing a category you just pushed.

If all you want is to put the right products on such a page (not rewrite its text), there is an easier way that skips this problem entirely: use a Category Override with Replace products turned on, then add just the correct items. That ignores Geiger's ~60 wrong products from the start, so only your items show.

7. Push to Sanity (the takeover tool)

This is the tab at the very top of the Studio. Its only job: let you take over an existing category page so you can edit it. It saves you from rebuilding the page by hand.

  1. Click the Push to Sanity tab at the top.
  2. Search for the category you want (by name or slug, for example "backpacks").
  3. Click Push to Sanity on that result.
  4. It creates a Custom Category document already filled in with that page's current heading, meta, intro, buying guide, FAQs, and products.
  5. Go to Content › Custom Category, open the new document, edit what you want, review the product list (see the warning above), and Publish.

From then on, that web address is served from your edited version. If you ever want the original automatic version back, delete or unpublish the Custom Category.

8. Generate with AI (the writing helper)

This is a button that appears only on Custom Category documents (you will see it among the document's actions, near the Publish button). It uses AI to draft page content for you.

Similar "Generate … with AI" buttons exist on Blog Post, Video, Page, Landing Page, and Product Page — each is described in its own section. They all work the same way: you provide a title (and optionally keywords), the AI drafts, you review and Publish.

  • What it fills: the intro paragraph, the buying-guide body, and the FAQs, written for promotional-products buyers.
  • What it does NOT do: it does not choose products for you, and it does not publish. You stay in control.
  1. Open a Custom Category document.
  2. Click Generate with AI.
  3. A small window with a spinner opens while it writes the intro, body, and FAQs (usually 20 to 40 seconds) and closes by itself when the fields fill in. You can close it early — generation keeps running.
  4. Read and edit the result so it sounds right for you.
  5. Set the product list yourself, then Publish.
Always review AI text

The AI is a helpful first draft, not a final answer. Read it before publishing, fix anything that is generic or inaccurate, and make sure the products and claims match what you actually offer.

9. Category Override (a light touch-up)

Use this when you want to tweak an existing category page's products without taking the whole page over. Open Content › Category Override and Create new.

  • Category Slug: search and pick the page you want to affect (this writes the exact address so a typo cannot point at nothing).
  • Force CTA (hide product grid): turn on to make the page always show the lead/contact form instead of products. Highest priority.
  • Force Products (always show grid): turn on to make a page show products even if the automatic rule would have hidden them. (Ignored if Force CTA is also on.)
  • Replace products (show only what I add): turn on to ignore the products Geiger files under this page and show only the SKUs and Custom Products you add below. This is the fix for an empty or off-topic page where Geiger returns the wrong items (for example a page that was showing ~60 unrelated products). Turn it on, add the correct products in Added SKUs / Added Products, and Publish, only your items show. Hidden SKUs still apply. If you turn it on but add nothing, the page shows the contact form instead. (You do not also need Force Products when using this; a non-empty replaced list shows the grid on its own.)
  • Hidden SKUs: search and add products you want removed from this page's grid.
  • Added SKUs: search and add existing Geiger products you want added to this page, even if Geiger does not file them under it.
  • Added Products: add your own products to this page — a Custom Product (a card that links out to another website) or a Product Page (a card that links to that product's own /products/… page on your site, with its price range and first photo). Both are pinned to the front of the grid and participate in the page's filters. (Tip: for Product Pages the easier route is usually the product's own "Add to categories" field — same result, and a product attached both ways still shows once.)

Publish, and the page reflects your tweaks while keeping its original heading, text, and FAQs. This is the recommended way to fix an "off-topic" category without rewriting it: hide the wrong items, turn on Force CTA to show the contact form, or, for an empty/off-topic page where the whole product set is wrong, turn on Replace products and add just the correct items.

Filters on a Replace-products page. When Replace products is on, the sidebar filters (Color, Material, Brand, Feature, Type, Made in USA, Eco, Closeout) reflect the products you added, and clicking a filter stays on that category page instead of jumping to the old Geiger product list. Existing Geiger SKUs you add bring all their filters automatically. For Custom Products (your own non-Geiger items), the filters follow the optional fields on the Custom Product: fill in Colors, Material, Brand, Features, or Types (use the same value names Geiger uses, for example "Blue", "Stainless Steel", "With A Lid"), and switch on Made in USA, Eco-Friendly, or Closeout to have the product included by those filters. Anything you leave blank simply skips that filter. An added Product Page participates the same way — its colors come from its color variants automatically, and Material / Features / Types / the three toggles come from its Filters section.

10. Product Placement (one product, many pages)

Category Override works from the page's side ("on this page, add/remove these products"). Product Placement works from the product's side ("this product should appear on these pages"). Use it when one product needs to show up in several categories at once. Open Content › Product Placement and Create new.

  • SKU: search and pick the one product (you will see its name confirmed as a preview).
  • Add to categories: pick all the category pages this product should appear on.
  • Remove from categories: pick any category pages it should be taken off.

Publish, and that product is added to (or removed from) every page you listed. If a page is named in both an Override and a Placement, removal always wins (a product told to be removed stays removed).

11. Custom Product (products you add yourself)

Your category pages normally pull products from Geiger automatically. A Custom Product is something you add by hand, for example an item from a different vendor, or anything you want to feature beyond the auto-pulled catalog. Open Content › Custom Product and Create new.

  • Title, image, description: the basics shown on the product card.
  • External URL: where the card links (any vendor; your own Geiger links are handled automatically).
  • Parent category: the category page this product should appear on.
  • Display order: where it sits in the grid.
  • Placements: toggles to also show it on Deals, New Products, and/or Rush Products.
  • Optional details (only needed for the Deals/New/Rush pages): brand, prices, minimum quantity, production time, and "new/sale/closeout" badges.
  • Optional filter fields — all optional; tag them to make the product show up under that filter on /deals, /new-products, /rush-products, and on any Replace-products category page; leave blank to skip that filter. Colors, Material, Features, and Types are word tags (use the same words Geiger uses, for example "Blue", "Stainless Steel", "With A Lid"); Made in USA, Eco-Friendly, and Closeout are simple on/off switches. There is no separate "New Item" switch — the NEW badge (or "Show on /new-products") already puts the product under the New Items filter, and the CLOSEOUT badge also counts for the Closeout filter.

A Custom Product can be attached to a category in three ways: its own Parent category field, an Category Override "Added Products", or a Custom Category's product list.

13. Page (the website builder)

The Page collection powers your non-category pages: the Services pages (Kitting, Company Stores, Popup Stores, 100% Custom Products) and your legal/info pages (About, Contact, Sample Policy, Shipping, Returns, Privacy & Security, Company Core Values, Terms). Open Content › Page and pick the page you want.

Each Page has a Title, a Slug (its address, for example kitting becomes /services/kitting), an SEO section, and a list of Sections. The Sections list is your website builder: drag to reorder, add a new section, delete one, or hide one without deleting it.

The section types you can add

Section What it is
Hero Banner A heading + subheading with a banner image and an optional button. A toggle controls whether the text sits on top of the image or above it.
Rich Text A block of formatted text: headings, sub-headings, paragraphs, bullet & numbered lists, bold/italic, quotes, and links. You can also drop an image inline between paragraphs (use the insert menu inside the text box). Can be made into an in-page jump target.
Image + Text A heading, a full-width image, then text below it (stacked vertically).
Infographic A full-width image with an optional caption.
Icon Feature Columns 2, 3, or 4 columns, each with an icon, heading, and short text.
Stat Banner A colored band highlighting a number or stat.
Card Grid A grid of cards, each with an optional image and button.
CTA Block A heading with one or more call-to-action buttons.
Event List Rows of city / venue / date / time (for trade shows or events).
Video Embed Paste a YouTube, YouTube Shorts, Vimeo, Instagram, or Facebook link and it plays in a responsive player right on the page. The correct player is detected automatically — you do not pick a provider. Optional heading and caption. (The video stays on its source platform; nothing is uploaded here.)
FAQ Accordion Question-and-answer pairs that expand and collapse.
Product Strip A live row of product cards on any page. Add products by Geiger SKU (or fill the manual title/image/link fields for a non-Geiger product) — prices, images, and affiliate links pull from the catalog automatically, so nothing goes stale. Optional heading. This is also the strip the Generate Page with AI helper pre-fills.

Every section has a Hidden toggle, so you can take a section off the live page and keep it for later. Image fields let you upload a file (preferred) or, in some, paste an image URL. After editing, Publish.

Good to know

Some Services pages may currently be drafts with placeholder text and empty image slots, waiting for your real copy and images. Fill those in and Publish when you are ready. The page addresses already match your site's menu, so you do not need to set up any links.

Generate Page with AI

Pages have their own AI writing helper, like Blog Posts and Videos do. Type a page Title, optionally add a short brief and a few keywords, click one button, and it drafts a full set of sections for you to review — it saves a draft and never publishes anything on its own.

  1. Create a new Page (or open one) and enter the Title — this is the main input, so the button stays disabled until it is filled.
  2. Optionally, in the AI generation section, add a Page brief (a sentence or two on what the page is about) and Topic Keywords (a few plural phrases like "custom water bottles" — these steer the product strip and the internal links).
  3. Click Generate Page with AI (among the document actions, near Publish). A window with a spinner opens while it writes — it usually takes under two minutes — and closes by itself when the draft fills in (closing it early is fine; generation keeps running). If it comes back with an error saying the result was thin, just click it again.
  4. Review everything. The draft gets: a text hero (heading + subheading + a button pointing to your contact form), several body text sections with internal links placed into the text automatically (the Suggested Internal Links list records everything it found and says which were placed), a pre-filled Product Strip, a stat banner, an FAQ section, and a closing call-to-action. The meta title/description fill in too. Reorder, edit, hide, or delete any section.
  5. Add your images and check the buttons. The AI writes text sections only — it never picks images, so upload the hero/banner images yourself (and add Image or Infographic sections if you want them). The hero and CTA buttons point to /contact by default; repoint them if you prefer.
  6. When it reads right, Publish.
Nothing gets overwritten

Generating appends its sections to the end of the Sections list — it never wipes or replaces sections that are already on the page. That also means clicking Generate a second time adds a second set of sections; just delete the set you do not want.

Always review AI text

The AI writes a first draft, not a final page. Read it before publishing, fix anything generic or inaccurate, and check that the product strip actually fits the topic (if nothing sufficiently relevant was found, the strip is simply left out — you can add one yourself and fill it by SKU).

Creating a brand-new page at your own address (recreate an old URL)

You are no longer limited to the Services and legal pages. You can create a brand-new Page and it will go live at its own top-level address, /your-slug. This is the way to recreate an old-site URL so you keep its Google ranking. Example: a Page with the slug llm-info-perfect-imprints goes live at /llm-info-perfect-imprints.

To create one:

  1. Open Content › Page and click Create new.
  2. Enter a Title and set the Slug (its address — type it or let it fill from the title). The slug is the exact address, so my-page becomes /my-page.
  3. Add your Sections (the same website-builder blocks listed above) and fill in the SEO section (meta title + description).
  4. Click Publish. The page is live at /your-slug within seconds and is added to your sitemap automatically so Google can find it.
Live within seconds

Just like categories, publishing a Page updates the live site within a few seconds — you do not wait for a code deploy. Editing an existing page works the same way.

Reserved addresses you cannot use

A handful of slugs are already used by other parts of the site, so Studio will block you (with the message "This URL is reserved by the site; choose another slug") if you try to save a Page at one of them:

cat, blog, videos, brands, deals, new-products, rush-products, rush-promotional-products, promotional-products, faq, search, services, admin3773752, api, about, contact, terms, privacy-security, returns, shipping-policy, sample-policy, company-core-values.

Just pick a different slug. (The Services pages keep using kitting, company-stores, popup-stores, custom-products — those stay at their /services/… addresses.)

Linking a new page into your menus (a separate, optional step)

Creating and publishing a page makes it live and findable by its address and by Google, but it does not automatically add it to any menu. Adding it to the top navigation or the footer is a separate step you do afterward, in a different part of Studio — the Page document itself has no "add to menu" switch, because the menus are their own documents. Think of it as two steps:

  1. Step 1 — Create the page (required). Content › Page › Create new → Title + Slug + Sections + SEO → Publish. The page is now live at /your-slug and in the sitemap. It is not yet in any menu, but anyone with the link — and Google — can already reach it.
  2. Step 2 — Link it into navigation (optional). Only do this if you want the page to appear in the site's menus or footer. Go to a different collection and paste the page's slug (for example /llm-info-perfect-imprints) as the link address:
    • Top navigation / mega menu: open Content › Mega Menu and add a new menu item, dropdown link, or panel-column link pointing to /your-slug (or add it to an existing dropdown/panel).
    • Footer: open Content › Global Settings and add a link (label + address) to one of the footer columns.
    No code change is needed for either — the menus and footer are all edited here in Studio, and they update live within seconds when you Publish.
Key point

The page works and is reachable by its URL (and by Google) without Step 2. Step 2 is only about making it visible inside the site's menus/footer.

14. Blog Post, Blog Category, Author

These three work together for your blog (around 649 posts already migrated).

Blog Post

Open Content › Blog Post to edit an existing post or create a new one. A post has a title, slug (its address under /blog/), header image, the body (rich text), an author, and a blog category. This field changes only the CTA block's heading. The "See Related Blogs About …" heading further down always uses the automatic category-based wording, and the AI blog generator never fills this field — it stays blank unless you type something.

Generate a Blog Post with AI

Blog Posts have their own AI writing helper, just like Custom Categories do (see Generate with AI). It saves a draft; it never publishes anything on its own.

  1. Create a new Blog Post (or open one you want to rewrite).
  2. Enter a Title — this is the topic the AI writes about.
  3. In the AI generation section, pick a Template:
    • List-style — a "10 Ideas …" post with a row of matching products under each idea. Best for idea round-ups and giveaway lists.

  4. Fill Topic Keywords (a few plural phrases like "custom water bottles"). These steer the writing, the product rows, and the internal links.

  6. Set the Approximate word count (default 1500, roughly 1300 to 1900). This is a target, not an exact count — expect the post to land within about 15 percent of it.
  7. Click Generate Blog with AI (among the document actions, near Publish). A window with a spinner opens while it writes — a full post can take a minute or two — and closes by itself when the draft fills in (closing it early is fine; generation keeps running). If it comes back with an error saying the result was thin, just click it again.
  8. (The optional CTA Heading field is never filled by the AI — set it yourself only if the automatic "Order Custom [category] Today" wording reads wrong for this post.)

About the product rows: each section's row is matched to that section's topic (a power-bank idea shows power banks, a tote-bag idea shows tote bags), and no product repeats anywhere in the post. If nothing sufficiently relevant is found for a section, that section simply has no product row — that is intentional; you can always insert a Products Block there yourself.

The AI writes a first draft, not a final post. Read it before publishing, fix anything generic or inaccurate, and check that the suggested products actually fit each section.

19. Custom Schema (advanced SEO / structured data)

This is an optional, advanced tool. It lets you add extra structured data (also called "schema" or "JSON-LD") to any page on the site — a category page, a blog post, a video, the home page, an info page, anything. Structured data is invisible code that helps Google and AI tools understand a page. You do not need this for normal SEO; the site already adds the important schema for you automatically. Use this only when you want to add a specific extra type.

The big idea: no "push" needed

Unlike taking over a category page, you do not have to push or take over a page to add schema to it. Each Custom Schema is one small document that simply points at a page by its address. Make one for each page you want to add schema to.

Open Content › Custom Schema (Structured Data) and Create new.

  • Page URL (path): the page you are adding schema to. Start typing a category name to find a /cat/... page, or type any other path directly — for example /about, /blog/your-post, /videos/your-video, or just / for the home page.
  • Schema type (for AI): only used by the AI button below.
  • Details for AI: optional notes the AI should use.
  • JSON-LD blocks: the actual schema. You can add more than one block to the same page.

Two ways to add a schema block

Option 1 — paste it in. If you already have JSON-LD (for example from a free online "schema markup generator"), click Add item under JSON-LD blocks and paste it in. It must be valid JSON containing @context and @type; if it is broken, Sanity won't let you publish until you fix it.

Option 2 — let AI write it (easiest). No outside tool needed:

  1. Set the Page URL.
  2. Pick a Schema type from the dropdown (e.g. Web Application, Product, How-To, Event).
  3. Optionally fill in Details for AI (e.g. "free web-based app for designing custom mugs").
  4. Click Generate schema with AI (in the document actions, near the Publish button).
  5. A new JSON-LD block appears in the list. Read and edit it so the details are correct.
  6. Publish. The schema appears on that page within seconds.
Don't re-add schema the site already provides

The site automatically adds FAQ, Organization, Website, Breadcrumb, and Video schema on the right pages. Do not add those here — it would create duplicates. That is why they are not in the Schema type dropdown.

Always review AI output

The AI gives you a solid starting point, but check the values (prices, names, links, descriptions) before publishing. If you are unsure whether you need schema on a page, you can safely skip this tool — it is optional.

20. Site Refresh (update your product data)

This is a tab at the very top of the Studio (next to Push to Sanity). It is your one-click way to pull the newest product information from Geiger and put it live on your site — no GitHub, no developer, no waiting. You see five buttons, each with a short note and a status line that tells you whether it is idle, running, finished, or failed.

The five buttons

  • Refresh New Products — updates the New Products page with Geiger's latest arrivals. Takes about a minute or two.
  • Refresh Deals — updates the Deals page with the current sale and closeout items. About a minute or two.
  • Refresh Rush Products — updates the Rush Products page (24-hour rush items). About a minute or two.
  • Full Catalog Rebuild — the big one. It rebuilds your entire catalog (22,000+ category pages, every product, every brand logo). Use it occasionally, roughly once a month. It can take a few hours, and your most-visited pages re-warm automatically when it is done.
  • Warm All Pages — visits every category page on your live site (22,000+ pages) so each one loads fast for its first visitor. See the section just below for when to use it.
Nothing runs on a schedule anymore — you are in control

New Products, Deals, and Rush Products used to refresh automatically every Sunday. That schedule is now turned off to keep hosting costs in your hands: the data only refreshes when you press a button here. Press them as often or as rarely as you like — once a week is a good rhythm if you want to keep the old cadence. The Full Catalog Rebuild has never run on a schedule — it also only happens when you press it.

The "Warm All Pages" button

Your site has 22,000+ category pages. After a deploy, the most important ones (about 5,000) are prepared in advance so they load instantly; the rest get prepared automatically the first time someone visits them — that first visit is just slightly slower (well under a second), and after that the page stays fast. So most of the time you do not need this button at all.

Warm All Pages visits every single page for you, in advance, so no visitor ever gets that slightly slower first load. It runs for about 15–30 minutes against your live site (www.perfectimprints.com).

It can increase your hosting cost — use it occasionally

Because it touches all 22,000+ pages in one go, it uses real hosting resources, which is why it asks you to confirm before it starts. It is optional, and most useful right after a Full Catalog Rebuild or another big content change — that is when lots of pages go "cold" at once. There is no need to run it after small edits or on a routine basis.

While it runs you see the same status line as the other buttons (Queued → Running… → finished), and you can press Cancel to stop it — pages already warmed stay warm, and nothing on your site changes.

How a refresh works

  1. Press a button. The status line changes to Queued, then Running….
  2. When it finishes, if there was anything new, the change is published for you automatically and your site rebuilds. You do not need to approve anything in GitHub.
  3. If nothing changed since last time, it simply finishes with nothing to publish — that is normal.
Good to know — allow ~12–15 minutes after it finishes

The refresh job itself is quick — about a minute or two for the three product refreshes (New Products, Deals, Rush Products). But when it finds new data and publishes it, your site has to rebuild and redeploy before the change actually shows up. That rebuild happens automatically — you do not need to do anything — but it takes roughly 12–15 minutes AFTER the job finishes (the site runs two builds, about 7 minutes each). So the flow is: the button says it is done → then wait about 12–15 more minutes → then the new data is live.

If a refresh found nothing new, there is no publish and no rebuild, so there is nothing to wait for. And the Full Catalog Rebuild is different — that job runs for a few hours on its own, and the same 12–15 minute redeploy happens after it finishes too.

The Cancel button

While a refresh is running you will see a Cancel button. Pressing it safely stops the run, and — this is the important part — nothing on your site changes. The refresh only ever goes live as its very last step, so cancelling before then leaves your site exactly as it was. After you cancel, the status shows "Cancelled — no changes applied."

One honest exception

If a run has already finished the moment you press Cancel, it is too late to undo — the change has gone live. The panel will tell you this plainly instead of pretending to cancel.

Creating one

  2. Enter a Title (your own label, e.g. "Beach Towels Destin FL") and fill the AI generation inputs: City (Destin), State (FL), Product / topic (Custom Beach Towels), and the Local landmarks you want mentioned (e.g. Crab Island, Henderson Beach State Park, Destin Harbor, HarborWalk Village, Okaloosa County). Optionally add Topic Keywords to steer the product row and the internal links.
  3. Click Generate Landing Page with AI (among the document actions, near Publish — it stays disabled until City, State, and Product are filled). A spinner window opens while it writes; it usually takes under two minutes. If it comes back with an error (thin result, or it dropped one of your landmarks), just click Generate again.

The draft fills: the hero heading + subheading, a Local intro that names every landmark you listed (the tool refuses to accept a result that skipped one), the Options & ideas content (product options plus creative uses-around-town ideas), the Why Perfect Imprints trust text, FAQs, a pre-filled Related products row, and the meta title/description. Edit anything.

Every page should feel local

The quote form and its settings

Every landing page ends with a quote form (First/Last Name, Email, Phone, what they are looking for, Quantity Needed, Date Needed — the last two required — plus an optional artwork upload). Three fields on the document control it:

  • Quote form heading — the heading above the form. Blank uses "Request a Quote in {City}, {State}" automatically (the AI pre-fills this pattern; edit it freely — regenerating never overwrites your wording).
Customers get a confirmation email

On landing pages (and only on landing pages), the person who submits the form automatically receives a friendly confirmation email summarizing what they sent — so they know the request went through, and replies to it go to that page's lead recipient. Your other forms (the category "Find Products for Me" form, the contact page) behave exactly as before: no confirmation, leads to you.

Always review AI text

The AI writes a first draft, not a final page. Read it before publishing — especially any local claims — and check the product row fits the topic (if nothing sufficiently relevant was found, the row is simply left out; you can add products yourself by SKU under Related products).

22. Product Pages (your own product detail pages)

Content › Product Page creates a full product of your own with its own page on the website at /products/<slug> — a photo gallery with color swatches, a quantity-price table, a description, related products, and a green Get a Quote button. No cart, no checkout, no outside link: the button opens your quote form and the lead lands in Lead Submission.

Product Page vs Custom Product

These are two different things. A Custom Product is a simple card that links OUT to another website (it has a required External URL). A Product Page is a full product that lives ON your site with its own page. If you want people to land on your site and request a quote, use a Product Page.

Where these products show up

  • Their own page at /products/<slug> (indexable, in the sitemap, with Product schema for Google).
  • At the top of the New Products page (/new-products) — controlled by the "Show on /new-products" toggle under Visibility.
  • In site search (the magnifying glass) — the result opens your product page, not Geiger.
  • In Related Products carousels on other product pages.
  • Inside /cat/... category pages only where you attach it yourself — the easy way is the product's own "Add to categories" field (next bullet's step 7); the alternative is adding it under a Category Override's Added Products from the category's side. Either way it appears in that category's grid as a card linking to its /products/… page (never automatically — you choose the categories, and attaching it both ways still shows it once).

Creating one, step by step

  1. Basics: Title (e.g. "32 oz Voyager Travel Tumbler"), Slug (click Generate), optionally a Brand, and optionally an Item / SKU number — if you give the product a real item number, customers can find it by typing that number into the site search (all Geiger products are already searchable by their item numbers).
  2. Images & Colors: add one Color variant per color the product comes in. Each variant has a color name (e.g. "Navy Blue"), an optional swatch color (a hex code like #1E3A8A — this paints the little clickable circle), and up to 10 images. On the live page, visitors see the FIRST variant's photos and can click the other swatches to swap the whole gallery to that color's photos. If your product has no colors, skip variants and use Default images instead.
  3. Pricing: add up to 5 tiers (quantity + price each), e.g. 100 → $4.99, 250 → $4.49, 500 → $3.99. The page shows them as the QTY / PRICE table, and the product card price range comes from them automatically. Optionally add a Setup charge — a one-time flat fee (e.g. a screen-print setup) added on top of quantity × price in the on-page estimated total. Turn on On sale for the red "Sale!" badge, optionally with a percent-off and an expiry date.
  4. Details: the full description (bullets, bold, links, headings all work — and when you make a link, the link popover now has an "Open in new tab" on/off switch), decoration methods (each method can carry an optional per-unit upcharge, e.g. Pad Print +$0.50 — it is added to the unit price in the live estimate when the visitor picks that method; leave it blank for no upcharge), Sizes (just listed on the page — sizes do not change the price or the pictures), and production time. Don't want to write the description yourself? Use the AI helper (next heading).
  5. Logistics / carton (optional): units per carton, carton weight (lbs), carton width / height / depth (inches), and the FOB ships-from zip (plus optional city/state). Whatever you fill in shows as compact lines near the product details — e.g. "500 units per carton", "16 × 14 × 10 in / 27 lbs per carton", "Ships from Memphis, TN 38109" — and also feeds Google-Merchant-ready shipping structured data. Blank fields simply don't render.
  6. Filters (optional): tag Material / Features / Types and the Made-in-USA / Eco / Closeout toggles using the same names Geiger uses, so the product participates in the New Products page filters. Colors are taken from your color variants automatically.
  7. Show on category pages ("Add to categories"): search a category by title or slug and click to add it — multi-select, add as many as you like. If the category you want does not exist yet, the picker offers "Create new category page" right there (it creates a Custom Category at /cat/<slug> and selects it). Publish, and the product appears as a card on each chosen category page (linking to this product page) and joins that category's Color/Material filters. This is different from "Related category" below, which only fills the Related Products carousel — it does not put the product on any category page.
  8. Related products, videos & blogs: pick a related category and/or a few keywords and the Related Products carousel fills itself with matching products. Anything you add manually (a Geiger product by SKU, one of your Custom Products, or another Product Page) is pinned to the front. Below Related Products the page also shows a "Related Videos" strip and a "Related Blogs" strip: they fill automatically from the same keywords, and the Related videos / Related blogs fields let you hand-pick which ones appear (your picks come first). No matches → the strip simply doesn't render.
  9. Visibility & SEO: the New Products toggle, the Lead recipient email (see below), plus the usual Meta Title / Meta Description / social image.
  10. Press Publish — the page is live at /products/<your-slug> within seconds, and it appears in New Products and search shortly after.

Generate Product Details with AI (the writing helper)

Same idea as the AI helpers on blogs, videos, pages, and landing pages. Enter the Title (and optionally a few plural keywords in the collapsed AI generation section at the bottom, e.g. "custom travel tumblers"), then click Generate Product Details with AI in the menu next to Publish. In about 20–60 seconds it drafts, for your review:

  • The Product details description — B2B buying-guide-style copy (features, decoration and branding options, popular uses), with links to related pages on your own site already placed.
  • The Meta Title and Meta Description (SEO).
  • Related keywords + related category, decoration method suggestions, and matching Related videos / Related blogs for the strips — but only if you left those fields blank; anything you already picked is never overwritten. (AI-suggested decoration methods come without an upcharge — set the per-unit upcharge yourself if one applies.)
AI links open in a new tab

Internal links the AI places in a description default the "Open in new tab" switch to ON (this applies to AI-written Page and Landing Page bodies too). You can turn any of them off in the link popover while reviewing the draft; links you make by hand default to same-tab.

It never touches your facts

The AI writes words only. Your pricing tiers, images and color variants, sizes, brand, minimum quantity, production time, and lead recipient are never generated or changed — those stay yours. If you filled in material/colors/sizes first, the AI mentions them; otherwise it stays generic and invents nothing. As always: it drafts, you review, nothing publishes by itself.

Re-clicking regenerates the description and SEO meta (that is the point of the button), so if you hand-polished the description, copy it somewhere before regenerating.

The on-page configurator (what visitors see)

The product page works like the Geiger page you know. Every choice starts with a sensible default already selected — the quantity starts at your lowest pricing tier, and the first color / size / decoration are pre-picked (anything with only one option just shows as text). As the visitor changes the quantity, the page highlights the matching pricing tier in green, and the tier boxes themselves are clickable — clicking one sets the quantity to that tier's minimum. The live estimated total is quantity × (that tier's price + the selected decoration's per-unit upcharge, if any), plus your Setup charge if you set one. It is always labeled "Estimated total — final pricing confirmed in your quote" — there is no checkout, and no price is ever presented as final. Clicking a color swatch under the photos swaps the gallery AND records the color for the quote.

The Get a Quote form (and where its leads go)

The green Get a Quote button opens the quote form pre-filled with whatever the visitor configured — quantity, color, size, decoration, and the estimated total, plus a one-line summary like "Custom Bamboo Folding Fan — White, Small, 150 units, Pad Print." — all still editable. The visitor only types their contact details: First Name, Email, and Date Needed are required; Last Name, Phone, Company, Shipping Zip, Comments, and a logo/artwork upload are optional. When someone submits it:

  • The request is emailed to the address in that product's Lead recipient email field (Visibility & SEO). It starts as patrick@perfectimprints.com; change it per product if a different person should handle that product's quotes. Blank or mistyped falls back to Patrick automatically.
  • The email subject names the product ("Product Quote Request: …"), and the message includes a clear configuration block — quantity, color, size, decoration (shown with its per-unit upcharge when one applies, e.g. "Pad Print (+$0.50/unit)"), and the estimated total — plus every contact field and any attached artwork.
  • The customer automatically receives a confirmation email summarizing what they sent — same as the landing pages.
  • A record appears under Lead Submission with the company, shipping zip, comments, the quoted product, the selected color/size/decoration, the estimated total, and a "Lead sent to" note showing where the notification went.
Safe by design

The visitor's browser never chooses where the lead goes — the website looks up the recipient from the product itself, on the server. So the address you set in Studio is the only place quotes can be sent.

23. Bulk Upload Product Pages (import from a spreadsheet)

Instead of building Product Pages one by one, you can create or update up to 50 at a time from a spreadsheet. Open the Bulk Upload tab at the top of the Studio (next to Site Refresh), and follow the flow:

  1. Download the sample template (the link at the top of the panel). It is a .csv file with the exact column headings and one filled-in example row. You can edit it in Excel or Google Sheets and save as .csv or .xlsx — both work.
  2. Fill in your products — one row per product. Title is the only required column. Leave any other cell blank and that field is simply not set. You can also delete whole columns you don't need; only keep the headings spelled exactly like the template.
  3. Upload the file and press Preview. Nothing is saved yet — you get a table showing, for every row, whether it will CREATE a new product, UPDATE an existing one, or has an ERROR (with a plain-language note of what to fix). Misspelled column headings are listed too, so a typo never silently disappears.
  4. Press Apply. The products import as drafts — the panel shows progress and a final per-row summary. Nothing appears on the live site yet.
  5. Review and publish. Find the new drafts under Content › Product Page, open each one, adjust anything (you can run Generate Product Details with AI on them like any other product), and press Publish when it looks right.
Re-uploading is safe — no duplicates

Each product's web address (slug) is the key: the Slug column if you filled it in, otherwise it is made from the Title (e.g. "20 oz Travel Tumbler" → 20-oz-travel-tumbler). If a Product Page with that slug already exists — draft or published — the import updates it instead of creating a second one, and it only changes the columns you filled in. A blank cell never erases something you already wrote in Studio. So a good workflow is: import, notice a wrong price, fix the price cell in the sheet, and upload the same file again.

Images are downloaded for you

Image cells hold picture web addresses (URLs) — right-click a picture on the supplier's site and choose "Copy image address", or use a link from your own file storage. On import, each picture is downloaded and stored in Sanity, so your product pages never depend on someone else's server staying up. A picture that can't be downloaded is skipped with a note in the results (the product still imports) — add it by hand in Studio afterwards.

The columns, one by one

Single-value columns (each row = one product; all optional except Title):

  • Title (required) — the product name and page heading.
  • Slug — the web address after /products/. Leave blank to derive it from the Title. Lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only (anything else is cleaned up automatically).
  • Brand, Item SKU — same as the fields in Studio (the SKU makes the product findable by number in site search).
  • Description — plain text; a blank line inside the cell starts a new paragraph. Formatting (bullets, links, headings) is added later in Studio or via the AI helper.
  • Min Qty, Setup Charge, Production Time — numbers (production time is in days; writing 7 working days is understood as 7). A number cell that can't be read is simply left unset with a note — it never blocks the row.
  • On Sale, Show In New Products, Made In USA, Eco Friendly, Closeout — write yes or no (blank = not set; new products default to showing on /new-products).
  • Sale Percent Off — a number 1–99, only used with On Sale = yes.
  • Lead Recipient — the email that receives this product's quote requests (blank = patrick@perfectimprints.com).
  • Related Category — one category slug (e.g. water-bottles) that fills the Related Products carousel.
  • Add To Categories — comma-separated category slugs the product should appear ON (e.g. water-bottles, coffee-mugs). Slugs that don't exist yet are flagged as a warning — the import never creates category pages; create those from the product form's picker as usual.
  • Related Keywords — comma-separated plural keywords (steer the automatic related matching).
  • Material, Features, Types — filter tags; Features and Types take comma-separated lists. Use the same names Geiger uses.
  • Units Per Carton, Carton Weight, Carton Width / Height / Depth, FOB Zip / City / State — the optional logistics facts (weight in lbs, dimensions in inches).

Multi-value fields use numbered columns — the same heading with 1, 2, 3… Add as many numbered columns as you need (up to 10 sizes/colors/decorations/images, and up to 5 pricing tiers):

  • Size 1, Size 2, … — one size per column (e.g. S, M, or 20 oz). Sizes are listed on the page; they don't change price or pictures.
  • Tier 1 Qty + Tier 1 Price, Tier 2 Qty + Tier 2 Price, … — the quantity-price table. A tier needs BOTH cells; a half-filled tier is skipped with a warning.
  • Decoration 1 (+ Decoration 1 Upcharge), Decoration 2 … — decoration methods, each with an optional per-unit dollar upcharge (blank = no upcharge).
  • Image 1, Image 2, … — picture URLs for a product WITHOUT color variants (the "Default images").
  • Color 1 Name + Color 1 Images (+ Color 1 Swatch), Color 2 … — one set per color. Color N Images is a comma-separated list of picture URLs for that color (put the whole list in one cell), and the optional Color N Swatch is a hex color like #1E3A8A for the clickable circle. On the page, visitors click a swatch to swap the gallery to that color's photos — exactly like a hand-built product.
Good to know

Up to 50 product rows per upload (split bigger lists into several files) and files up to 4 MB. Prices can be written with a dollar sign ($4.99 works). One bad row never blocks the rest — it is skipped with a note while the other rows import. If you edit the file as raw text (not in Excel/Google Sheets), wrap any cell that contains a comma in double quotes — otherwise the row's columns shift and the preview flags it with a clear error. And because everything arrives as a draft, there is no risk to the live site: review, then publish.

24. Forms (build your own quote forms)

The Form document type is a form builder: you choose the questions, and the site renders a working form — with spam protection, the lead email to you, an automatic confirmation email to the customer, and a Lead Submission record — all handled for you. Four service quote forms are already set up (Kitting, Company Stores, 100% Custom Products, Pop-Up Stores), and you can build brand-new ones for future Service or Landing pages yourself.

Open Content › Form and Create new (or open one of the four seeded forms). The fields:

  • Title — the form's heading, also used in the subject line of the lead email you receive.
  • Slug — the form's id. Page buttons point at it, so avoid changing it after wiring a button.
  • Lead recipient email — where submissions are emailed. Defaults to patrick@perfectimprints.com; change it per form if someone else should get those leads. This address is kept server-side — it is never visible in the page code.
  • Email the customer a confirmation copy — on by default; the customer gets a friendly summary of what they sent.
  • Intro — optional blurb shown above the questions.
  • Fields — the questions, in order (drag to reorder). Each has a Label, a Field type (short text, paragraph, email, phone, number, date, dropdown, yes/no checkbox, checkbox group, or file upload), a Required toggle, and an optional Placeholder. Dropdowns and checkbox groups also take a list of Options.
  • Submit button label and Success message — the button text and the thank-you note shown after sending.
Putting a form on a page

Forms open as a popup from a page button. In any Page, edit a CTA Block button (or a Hero Banner's CTA) and fill its "Open a form instead (form slug)" field with the form's slug (e.g. kitting-quote). Clicking the button then opens that form instead of following the link. Keep the button's normal link filled in too — if the form is unpublished (or the slug has a typo), the button safely falls back to the link. The four Services pages' "Request a Quote" buttons are already wired this way.

Publish the form

A form only opens on the live site once it is published. The four seeded service forms start as drafts — review each one and press Publish. Until then, the "Request a Quote" buttons keep linking to the contact page.

Edits go live within seconds of publishing (add a question, change the recipient, reword the button). File uploads use the same rules as the contact form: up to 3 files, 10 MB each (PDF, PNG, JPG, GIF, SVG, AI, EPS), and the files arrive attached to your lead email and stored on the Lead Submission.

25. How everything connects

A quick mental map of what drives what:

  • Global Settings drives the header and footer of every page (contact details, social icons, footer link columns, copyright).
  • Home Page drives only the homepage.
  • Mega Menu drives the top navigation bar across the site.
  • Category pages (/cat/...) run automatically, and are shaped by the three tools: Custom Category (take over the whole page), Category Override (tweak its products), and Product Placement (push one product across many pages). Push to Sanity is how you start a takeover; Generate with AI helps write a Custom Category's text; Custom Product supplies non-Geiger items that any of these can place.
  • Blog Post connects to Author and Blog Category.
  • Page drives the Services and legal/info pages through the section builder.
  • Landing Page drives the local + topic sales pages (e.g. /custom-beach-towels-destin-fl) — a fixed template the AI fills from your City / State / Product / landmarks inputs.
  • Product Page drives your own product detail pages (/products/...) — full products with a gallery, tiered pricing, and a Get a Quote button. They also surface on New Products, in search, and in related-product carousels (never inside /cat category pages).
  • FAQ, Video, and Brand each drive their own page (/faq, /videos, /brands).
  • Lead Submission is the inbox for your contact form.
  • Custom Schema is an optional advanced tool that adds extra SEO structured data to any page, without taking the page over.
  • Site Refresh is the top-tab panel that pulls fresh product data from Geiger (Deals, New Products, Rush Products, or the full catalog) and publishes it for you — with a Cancel that safely stops a run with no changes. It also has the optional Warm All Pages button to pre-load every category page after a big rebuild.
  • Curated Category still shows in the list but no longer powers any page; ignore it.

27. Important warnings (keep these in mind)

1. Always review products before publishing a pushed category

When you use Push to Sanity on a category that did not have clean Geiger products (the "full-capped-60" ones, like belt-buckles), about 60 off-topic products get copied in and become visible once you publish. Review and clear the product list first, or turn on Force CTA / leave the external link blank so the page shows the contact form instead. (Full details in the Custom Category section.)

2. Nothing is live until you Publish

Editing saves a draft, but the website does not change until you press Publish. If a change "did not work," check whether you published it.

3. Always review AI-generated text

Generate with AI gives you a first draft. Read it, edit it, and make sure it is accurate before publishing.

4. Do not use Curated Category

It still appears in your Content list, but it no longer powers any page on the site. Leave it alone and use Custom Category instead.

5. Add links with the link button, not raw code

In rich-text fields, select the words and click the link button. Do not paste link code like <a href...>; it will show as plain text instead of a working link.
