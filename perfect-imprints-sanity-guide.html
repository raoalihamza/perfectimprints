1. Sanity basics (start here)

Your website has two halves. The public website is what your customers see. Sanity Studio is the private dashboard where you change the words, images, products, links, and pages. You do not touch any code. You fill in fields and click Publish, and the website updates by itself.

Opening the Studio and logging in

The Studio lives at a private web address (your developer will give you the exact link, it ends in /admin3773752). Open it in your browser and log in. Once you are in, you will see a list on the left called Content. That list is your table of contents for the whole website.

The Content list (the left menu)

Everything you can edit is one of the items in that left list: Home Page, Global Settings, Mega Menu, Custom Category, Category Override, Product Placement, Custom Product, Blog Post, Blog Category, Author, FAQ, Video, Brand, Lead Submission, Page (and one item, Curated Category, that you should ignore). The rest of this guide walks through each one.

Two kinds of items: singletons and collections

  • Singletons are one-of-a-kind pages. There is exactly one Home Page, one Global Settings, one Mega Menu. You click it and edit the one document.
  • Collections are lists you add to. Blog Post, Custom Category, Brand, FAQ, and so on each hold many documents. You click the menu item, then either open an existing document or click the + (or "Create new") to add another.

The single most important idea: Draft vs Published

Read this twice

When you edit something, your changes are saved as a Draft automatically, but the public website does not change yet. The website only updates when you press the blue Publish button (bottom-right of the document).

So the rule is simple: edit, then Publish. If you ever change something and "it didn't update on the site", 99% of the time it is because you forgot to Publish.

A document with unpublished changes usually shows a small "Draft" or orange marker. After you Publish, that marker clears. You can keep a draft as long as you like and Publish later, which is handy for preparing something before it goes live.

How a change reaches the live site

After you Publish, the website rebuilds the affected page automatically, usually within a minute. You do not need to call your developer, and nothing needs to be "deployed". Publish is all it takes.

Editing fields

Inside a document you will see labelled boxes (fields). Some are simple text, some are rich text (where you can make words bold or add a link), some are lists you can drag to reorder, and some are images. A few useful habits:

  • Rich text links: select the words you want to turn into a link, then click the link button in the little toolbar and type the address (for example /rush-products for a page on your own site, or a full https://... address for an outside site). Do not type the link as raw code like <a href...>; use the link button instead.
  • Images: most image fields let you either upload a file or, in some places, paste an image URL. Uploading is preferred. To swap an image, open the field and choose a new file.
  • Lists: drag the handle on the left of a row to reorder. Use the + or "Add item" to add. Click the three dots or the X to remove.
  • Hide instead of delete: in several places (the page builder especially) there is a "Hidden" toggle. Turning it on removes a section from the live page without deleting your work, so you can bring it back later.

The three tabs at the very top

  • Structure is the normal editing view (the Content list). You will be here almost all the time.
  • Vision is a technical query tool. Ignore it. You will never need it.
  • Push to Sanity is a special tool for taking over one of your existing category pages so you can edit it. It has its own section below (jump to it).

2. Home Page

This singleton controls your homepage, top to bottom. Open Content › Home Page. The fields, in roughly the order they appear on the page:

Hero (the big text at the top)

Three editable pieces: a small eyebrow line ("BULK PROMOTIONAL EXPERTS SINCE 1999"), the big headline ("Custom Promotional Products That People Actually Use"), and the sub-paragraph below it. Change the words here and Publish. (There is also an older image-banner field kept as a fallback; you do not need it.)

Banner Row heading + the three banners

Just under the hero is a row of up to three image banners (Sunglasses, Event Tents, Hats, by default). You can edit:

  • The heading above the row ("Featured Product Categories") and its sub-line.
  • Each banner: its image, the link it goes to (for example /cat/sunglasses), and its alt text. Swap any image, change any link, or remove a banner. An empty row simply shows nothing.

Value Pillars (the three feature boxes)

The boxes like "Bulk Pricing on 22,000+ Products" and "Rush Production Available". Each has a heading and a body. The body is rich text, so you can add links inside it (select the words, click the link button). If you ever add a fourth or more pillar, the row automatically turns into a rotating carousel showing three at a time.

Testimonials + heading

The customer quotes section. You can edit the heading ("What Our Customers are Saying") and the list of testimonials (each has the quote, the person's name, and their title/company). The section shows three at a time and scrolls/rotates automatically when there are more than three.

Section headings for the product rows

There are editable headings for the "New and Trending Promotional Products" row and the "Rush Production Promotional Products" row. The products in those rows are pulled in automatically; you control the headings here.

Bottom SEO text

The long paragraph block near the footer ("Perfect Imprints has been helping businesses..."). It is rich text and spans the full width; links inside it show in red.

Homepage meta title and description

The title and description Google shows for your homepage in search results. Pre-set to your approved copy; edit if you wish, keeping the title short (around 60 characters) and the description around 155 characters.

3. Global Settings

This singleton holds the things that appear across the whole site (header, footer, contact details). Change something here and it updates everywhere at once. Open Content › Global Settings.

Contact details + hours

Your phone number, email, mailing address, and hours of operation ("M-F 9:00 AM-5:00 PM EST"). These feed the footer, the contact areas, and the business information Google reads about you. Update them here in one place.

Social Links

Your social media profiles (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook, X, YouTube, Pinterest). For each one you set the platform, paste the profile URL, and toggle it on. The matching icon appears automatically. Turn one off and it disappears from the footer; there are no dead placeholder links.

Footer Columns (the three link columns)

The footer's three link columns (About Us, Popular Links, Customer Service) are fully editable here. Change a label, change where a link points, add or remove links, or reorder them. The Contact column and the social icons are handled separately (above), not in this list.

Copyright text

The line at the very bottom. Use (year) as a placeholder and it is replaced with the current year automatically.

CTA Banner

Default wording for the call-to-action banner used in places around the site (heading, body, button label and link).

Deals / New Products / Rush Products page controls

Each of these three aggregator pages has a small set of controls here:

  • Heading, intro paragraph, meta title and description for the page.
  • Hide these SKUs: paste a product's SKU to remove it from that page's grid.
  • Pin these SKUs: paste a SKU to force a product onto that page even if the automatic feed missed it.

The products on these three pages are refreshed automatically from Geiger every week. These controls let you nudge what shows without anyone touching the feed.

4. Mega Menu (the top navigation)

This singleton is your main website menu, the bar across the top (Shop by, All Categories, New Products, Rush Products, Deals, Brands, Services, Videos, Blog). Open Content › Mega Menu. The Items list is the menu, left to right; drag to reorder, and the live header follows.

Each item has a Type:

  • Plain link: a single clickable word with one address (New Products, Deals, Brands, Videos, Blog).
  • Dropdown (flat list): a simple drop-down of links (Services: Kitting, Company Stores, and so on).
  • Mega panel (columns): the big multi-column panels (Shop by, All Categories). These have columns, each with a header and a list of links.

You can rename items, change where they link, hide them, add new ones, or reorder them. For most day-to-day needs you will only ever tweak a label or a link here.

Note

The two big panels (Shop by, All Categories) already contain hundreds of category links generated from your full catalog. You can edit them, but you usually will not need to. Be careful not to delete a whole panel by accident.

5. Your category pages, and the three tools that control them

This is the most important section to understand, because it is where most of your day-to-day product work happens, and there are three related tools that can confuse newcomers. Read this overview first, then the three tools that follow.

How category pages work by default

You have about 22,180 category pages (like /cat/water-bottles or /cat/backpacks). They were generated automatically, with their heading, intro text, buying guide, FAQs, and product grid already built in. You do not need to touch most of them. They run themselves and pull live products from Geiger.

You only step in when you want to change one: edit its words, add or remove specific products, take it over completely, or build a brand-new category page that does not exist yet. That is what the three tools are for.

The three tools at a glance

Tool Think of it as Use it when you want to…
Custom Category Taking over (or creating) a whole page Edit the words, heading, intro, buying guide, FAQs, and product list of a page, or build a brand-new category page from scratch.
Category Override A light touch-up on one existing page Just add or remove a few products on a page, or force a page to show the contact form instead of products, without rewriting the whole page.
Product Placement Moving one product around Put one specific product into several categories at once (or remove it from several), working from the product's side instead of the page's side.
Simple rule of thumb

Editing the page's words/content? Use Custom Category (usually via Push to Sanity).
Just adding or removing a few products on one page? Use Category Override.
One product needs to appear in many pages? Use Product Placement.

6. Custom Category (take over or create a page)

A Custom Category document is a full category page that you control. When you publish one, it takes over its web address (for example, a Custom Category with slug backpacks takes over /cat/backpacks) and the page is now driven by what you typed, not the automatic version. Delete or unpublish it, and the page falls back to the automatic version. Nothing is lost.

Two ways to make one:

  • To take over a page that already exists (like /cat/backpacks): use the Push to Sanity tool. It creates the Custom Category for you already filled in with the current heading, text, FAQs, and products, so you start from what is live and just edit. This is the recommended path.
  • To build a brand-new page that does not exist yet: open Content › Custom Category, click Create new, and fill it in from scratch.

The fields you will see

  • Title / heading (H1): the big page title.
  • Slug: the web address segment after /cat/. Pick carefully; this is the page's address.
  • SEO (meta title and description): what Google shows in search results.
  • Intro: the lead-in paragraph under the heading (rich text).
  • Body / buying guide: the longer content lower on the page (rich text, with headings).
  • FAQs: question-and-answer pairs that appear on the page (and help your SEO).
  • Products: the product list for the page. You add products with the searchable picker (type a product name, SKU, or brand and click to add). You can also add Custom Products here.
  • External link (optional): if set, the page's buttons point there; if left blank, buttons point to your contact form.

Editing, then publishing

  1. Open the Custom Category (or create it via Push to Sanity).
  2. Edit any fields you want: heading, intro, buying guide, FAQs, products.
  3. Optionally use the Generate with AI button to draft the text for you.
  4. Review everything, especially the product list.
  5. Click Publish. The page is now live with your version.
Important: the "full-capped-60" categories

A small number of categories (around 65, such as belt-buckles) do not have a clean set of Geiger products, so the automatic system quietly hid an off-topic fallback set of about 60 products behind a "show the contact form instead" rule. That is fine while the page runs automatically.

But the moment you take such a page over with Push to Sanity, those ~60 off-topic products get copied into your product list and the "hide" rule no longer applies, so they become visible. Before you Publish a page like this, look through the Products list and remove anything off-topic (or turn on Force CTA / leave the external link blank so it shows the contact form instead). Always review the product list before publishing a category you just pushed.

If all you want is to put the right products on such a page (not rewrite its text), there is an easier way that skips this problem entirely: use a Category Override with Replace products turned on, then add just the correct items. That ignores Geiger's ~60 wrong products from the start, so only your items show.

7. Push to Sanity (the takeover tool)

This is the tab at the very top of the Studio. Its only job: let you take over an existing category page so you can edit it. It saves you from rebuilding the page by hand.

  1. Click the Push to Sanity tab at the top.
  2. Search for the category you want (by name or slug, for example "backpacks").
  3. Click Push to Sanity on that result.
  4. It creates a Custom Category document already filled in with that page's current heading, meta, intro, buying guide, FAQs, and products.
  5. Go to Content › Custom Category, open the new document, edit what you want, review the product list (see the warning above), and Publish.

From then on, that web address is served from your edited version. If you ever want the original automatic version back, delete or unpublish the Custom Category.

8. Generate with AI (the writing helper)

This is a button that appears only on Custom Category documents (you will see it among the document's actions, near the Publish button). It uses AI to draft page content for you.

  • What it fills: the intro paragraph, the buying-guide body, and the FAQs, written for promotional-products buyers.
  • What it does NOT do: it does not choose products for you, and it does not publish. You stay in control.
  1. Open a Custom Category document.
  2. Click Generate with AI.
  3. Wait a few seconds while it writes the intro, body, and FAQs.
  4. Read and edit the result so it sounds right for you.
  5. Set the product list yourself, then Publish.
Always review AI text

The AI is a helpful first draft, not a final answer. Read it before publishing, fix anything that is generic or inaccurate, and make sure the products and claims match what you actually offer.

9. Category Override (a light touch-up)

Use this when you want to tweak an existing category page's products without taking the whole page over. Open Content › Category Override and Create new.

  • Category Slug: search and pick the page you want to affect (this writes the exact address so a typo cannot point at nothing).
  • Force CTA (hide product grid): turn on to make the page always show the lead/contact form instead of products. Highest priority.
  • Force Products (always show grid): turn on to make a page show products even if the automatic rule would have hidden them. (Ignored if Force CTA is also on.)
  • Replace products (show only what I add): turn on to ignore the products Geiger files under this page and show only the SKUs and Custom Products you add below. This is the fix for an empty or off-topic page where Geiger returns the wrong items (for example a page that was showing ~60 unrelated products). Turn it on, add the correct products in Added SKUs / Added Products, and Publish, only your items show. Hidden SKUs still apply. If you turn it on but add nothing, the page shows the contact form instead. (You do not also need Force Products when using this; a non-empty replaced list shows the grid on its own.)
  • Hidden SKUs: search and add products you want removed from this page's grid.
  • Added SKUs: search and add existing Geiger products you want added to this page, even if Geiger does not file them under it.
  • Added Products: add your own Custom Products to this page.

Publish, and the page reflects your tweaks while keeping its original heading, text, and FAQs. This is the recommended way to fix an "off-topic" category without rewriting it: hide the wrong items, turn on Force CTA to show the contact form, or, for an empty/off-topic page where the whole product set is wrong, turn on Replace products and add just the correct items.

10. Product Placement (one product, many pages)

Category Override works from the page's side ("on this page, add/remove these products"). Product Placement works from the product's side ("this product should appear on these pages"). Use it when one product needs to show up in several categories at once. Open Content › Product Placement and Create new.

  • SKU: search and pick the one product (you will see its name confirmed as a preview).
  • Add to categories: pick all the category pages this product should appear on.
  • Remove from categories: pick any category pages it should be taken off.

Publish, and that product is added to (or removed from) every page you listed. If a page is named in both an Override and a Placement, removal always wins (a product told to be removed stays removed).

11. Custom Product (products you add yourself)

Your category pages normally pull products from Geiger automatically. A Custom Product is something you add by hand, for example an item from a different vendor, or anything you want to feature beyond the auto-pulled catalog. Open Content › Custom Product and Create new.

  • Title, image, description: the basics shown on the product card.
  • External URL: where the card links (any vendor; your own Geiger links are handled automatically).
  • Parent category: the category page this product should appear on.
  • Display order: where it sits in the grid.
  • Placements: toggles to also show it on Deals, New Products, and/or Rush Products.
  • Optional details (only needed for the Deals/New/Rush pages): brand, prices, minimum quantity, production time, colors, material, and "new/sale/closeout" badges. If you fill in colors and material using the same words Geiger uses, your custom product joins the filters alongside the Geiger products.

A Custom Product can be attached to a category in three ways: its own Parent category field, an Category Override "Added Products", or a Custom Category's product list.

13. Page (the website builder)

The Page collection powers your non-category pages: the Services pages (Kitting, Company Stores, Popup Stores, 100% Custom Products) and your legal/info pages (About, Contact, Sample Policy, Shipping, Returns, Privacy & Security, Company Core Values, Terms). Open Content › Page and pick the page you want.

Each Page has a Title, a Slug (its address, for example kitting becomes /services/kitting), an SEO section, and a list of Sections. The Sections list is your website builder: drag to reorder, add a new section, delete one, or hide one without deleting it.

The section types you can add

Section What it is
Hero Banner A heading + subheading with a banner image and an optional button. A toggle controls whether the text sits on top of the image or above it.
Rich Text A block of formatted text (with optional heading and links). Can also be made into an in-page jump target.
Image + Text A heading, a full-width image, then text below it (stacked vertically).
Infographic A full-width image with an optional caption.
Icon Feature Columns 2, 3, or 4 columns, each with an icon, heading, and short text.
Stat Banner A colored band highlighting a number or stat.
Card Grid A grid of cards, each with an optional image and button.
CTA Block A heading with one or more call-to-action buttons.
Event List Rows of city / venue / date / time (for trade shows or events).
FAQ Accordion Question-and-answer pairs that expand and collapse.

Every section has a Hidden toggle, so you can take a section off the live page and keep it for later. Image fields let you upload a file (preferred) or, in some, paste an image URL. After editing, Publish.

Good to know

Some Services pages may currently be drafts with placeholder text and empty image slots, waiting for your real copy and images. Fill those in and Publish when you are ready. The page addresses already match your site's menu, so you do not need to set up any links.

14. Blog Post, Blog Category, Author

These three work together for your blog (around 649 posts already migrated).

Blog Post

19. Custom Schema (advanced SEO / structured data)

This is an optional, advanced tool. It lets you add extra structured data (also called "schema" or "JSON-LD") to any page on the site — a category page, a blog post, a video, the home page, an info page, anything. Structured data is invisible code that helps Google and AI tools understand a page. You do not need this for normal SEO; the site already adds the important schema for you automatically. Use this only when you want to add a specific extra type.

The big idea: no "push" needed

Unlike taking over a category page, you do not have to push or take over a page to add schema to it. Each Custom Schema is one small document that simply points at a page by its address. Make one for each page you want to add schema to.

Open Content › Custom Schema (Structured Data) and Create new.

  • Page URL (path): the page you are adding schema to. Start typing a category name to find a /cat/... page, or type any other path directly — for example /about, /blog/your-post, /videos/your-video, or just / for the home page.
  • Schema type (for AI): only used by the AI button below.
  • Details for AI: optional notes the AI should use.
  • JSON-LD blocks: the actual schema. You can add more than one block to the same page.

Two ways to add a schema block

Option 1 — paste it in. If you already have JSON-LD (for example from a free online "schema markup generator"), click Add item under JSON-LD blocks and paste it in. It must be valid JSON containing @context and @type; if it is broken, Sanity won't let you publish until you fix it.

Option 2 — let AI write it (easiest). No outside tool needed:

  1. Set the Page URL.
  2. Pick a Schema type from the dropdown (e.g. Web Application, Product, How-To, Event).
  3. Optionally fill in Details for AI (e.g. "free web-based app for designing custom mugs").
  4. Click Generate schema with AI (in the document actions, near the Publish button).
  5. A new JSON-LD block appears in the list. Read and edit it so the details are correct.
  6. Publish. The schema appears on that page within seconds.
Don't re-add schema the site already provides

The site automatically adds FAQ, Organization, Website, Breadcrumb, and Video schema on the right pages. Do not add those here — it would create duplicates. That is why they are not in the Schema type dropdown.

Always review AI output

The AI gives you a solid starting point, but check the values (prices, names, links, descriptions) before publishing. If you are unsure whether you need schema on a page, you can safely skip this tool — it is optional.

20. Site Refresh (update your product data)

This is a tab at the very top of the Studio (next to Push to Sanity). It is your one-click way to pull the newest product information from Geiger and put it live on your site — no GitHub, no developer, no waiting. You see four buttons, each with a short note and a status line that tells you whether it is idle, running, finished, or failed.

The four buttons

  • Refresh New Products — updates the New Products page with Geiger's latest arrivals. Takes about a minute or two.
  • Refresh Deals — updates the Deals page with the current sale and closeout items. About a minute or two.
  • Refresh Rush Products — updates the Rush Products page (24-hour rush items). About a minute or two.
  • Full Catalog Rebuild — the big one. It rebuilds your entire catalog (22,000+ category pages, every product, every brand logo). Use it occasionally, roughly once a month. It can take a few hours, and your pages re-warm automatically when it is done, so you do not need to do anything else.
The three weekly ones run on their own too

New Products, Deals, and Rush Products already refresh automatically every Sunday. The buttons here are just for when you want fresh data sooner than the next Sunday. The Full Catalog Rebuild does not run on a schedule — it only happens when you press it.

How a refresh works

  1. Press a button. The status line changes to Queued, then Running….
  2. When it finishes, if there was anything new, the change is published for you automatically and your site rebuilds. You do not need to approve anything in GitHub.
  3. If nothing changed since last time, it simply finishes with nothing to publish — that is normal.
Good to know — allow ~12–15 minutes after it finishes

The refresh job itself is quick — about a minute or two for the weekly ones (New Products, Deals, Rush Products). But when it finds new data and publishes it, your site has to rebuild and redeploy before the change actually shows up. That rebuild happens automatically — you do not need to do anything — but it takes roughly 12–15 minutes AFTER the job finishes (the site runs two builds, about 7 minutes each). So the flow is: the button says it is done → then wait about 12–15 more minutes → then the new data is live.

If a refresh found nothing new, there is no publish and no rebuild, so there is nothing to wait for. And the Full Catalog Rebuild is different — that job runs for a few hours on its own, and the same 12–15 minute redeploy happens after it finishes too.

The Cancel button

While a refresh is running you will see a Cancel button. Pressing it safely stops the run, and — this is the important part — nothing on your site changes. The refresh only ever goes live as its very last step, so cancelling before then leaves your site exactly as it was. After you cancel, the status shows "Cancelled — no changes applied."

One honest exception

If a run has already finished the moment you press Cancel, it is too late to undo — the change has gone live. The panel will tell you this plainly instead of pretending to cancel.

21. How everything connects

A quick mental map of what drives what:

  • Global Settings drives the header and footer of every page (contact details, social icons, footer link columns, copyright).
  • Home Page drives only the homepage.
  • Mega Menu drives the top navigation bar across the site.
  • Category pages (/cat/...) run automatically, and are shaped by the three tools: Custom Category (take over the whole page), Category Override (tweak its products), and Product Placement (push one product across many pages). Push to Sanity is how you start a takeover; Generate with AI helps write a Custom Category's text; Custom Product supplies non-Geiger items that any of these can place.
  • Blog Post connects to Author and Blog Category.
  • Page drives the Services and legal/info pages through the section builder.
  • FAQ, Video, and Brand each drive their own page (/faq, /videos, /brands).
  • Lead Submission is the inbox for your contact form.
  • Custom Schema is an optional advanced tool that adds extra SEO structured data to any page, without taking the page over.
  • Site Refresh is the top-tab panel that pulls fresh product data from Geiger (Deals, New Products, Rush Products, or the full catalog) and publishes it for you — with a Cancel that safely stops a run with no changes.
  • Curated Category still shows in the list but no longer powers any page; ignore it.

23. Important warnings (keep these in mind)

1. Always review products before publishing a pushed category

When you use Push to Sanity on a category that did not have clean Geiger products (the "full-capped-60" ones, like belt-buckles), about 60 off-topic products get copied in and become visible once you publish. Review and clear the product list first, or turn on Force CTA / leave the external link blank so the page shows the contact form instead. (Full details in the Custom Category section.)

2. Nothing is live until you Publish

Editing saves a draft, but the website does not change until you press Publish. If a change "did not work," check whether you published it.

3. Always review AI-generated text

Generate with AI gives you a first draft. Read it, edit it, and make sure it is accurate before publishing.

4. Do not use Curated Category

It still appears in your Content list, but it no longer powers any page on the site. Leave it alone and use Custom Category instead.

5. Add links with the link button, not raw code

In rich-text fields, select the words and click the link button. Do not paste link code like <a href...>; it will show as plain text instead of a working link.
